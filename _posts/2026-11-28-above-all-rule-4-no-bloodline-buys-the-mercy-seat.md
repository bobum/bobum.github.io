*Part four of seven. Last week I argued that a substantially Christian people will naturally come to express its convictions in its public life. That argument is worth very little if it cannot be distinguished from a very different claim, often made in the same vocabulary, that a particular ancestry carries a superior standing before God. The distinction cannot be made by assertion alone, so I want to state it carefully.*

## Christian Nationalism Is Not Ethnic Supremacy

One objection to Christian nationalism is that it can become entangled with ethnic nationalism, treating a particular ancestry as though it confers a superior claim to a nation or to God's favor. That danger is real, but it is not inherent in the conviction that a nation should acknowledge Christ.

I do not believe any tribe possesses an inherent spiritual superiority over another. No bloodline purchases a place before God, and no ancestry gives anyone a claim on the mercy seat. We are all sinners in need of the same grace, and salvation is found in Jesus Christ rather than in our genealogy.

The church itself testifies to this reality. Christ is gathering a people from every tribe, tongue, people, and nation. Our unity in Him does not depend on shared ethnicity, and no earthly ancestry places someone closer to the throne of grace.

That conviction should shape how Christians think about the nation. If a nation is to be Christian, its defining characteristic should be its acknowledgment of Christ and its commitment to ordering public life according to Christian convictions—not the supposed superiority of a particular bloodline.

This does not mean that national borders, citizenship, cultural inheritance, or a government's responsibility toward its own population are meaningless. Those are legitimate subjects for political and theological discussion. But they must not be turned into claims of racial superiority or hereditary spiritual privilege. The line between the two is worth drawing carefully, and it falls where ancestry and culture part company.

Christianity cannot be reduced to an ethnic inheritance. One can possess a Christian ancestry without possessing Christian faith, just as someone from a people with no historical Christian majority can be brought into the kingdom through the gospel.

The relevant question is not which bloodline has the strongest claim to a nation. It is whether the people of that nation acknowledge the authority of Jesus Christ.

A Christian political vision must take seriously both the particular responsibilities of civil government and the universal scope of the gospel. It must reject the notion that ethnicity confers spiritual rank while recognizing that Christ's authority extends over every people and every nation.

## One Race, Many Nations, and the Question of Culture

Scripture does not divide humanity into races. It speaks of nations, tribes, tongues, and families. Paul told the Athenians that God made from one man every nation of mankind to live on all the face of the earth, having determined allotted periods and the boundaries of their dwelling place (Acts 17:26). That verse denies any plurality of human stocks and affirms that distinct nations, with real boundaries, are God's own arrangement rather than an accident to be abolished. The modern notion of race, a handful of biologically distinct human kinds, is a recent invention, not a biblical category.

This takes a distinctly Reformed form. Total depravity levels everyone. If every human being is radically fallen and unable to save himself, no people possesses moral altitude over another. Israel is the one nation in history with a genuinely divine ancestral claim, and Israel was told repeatedly that the ancestry secured nothing. Paul writes that not all who are descended from Israel belong to Israel (Romans 9:6). John the Baptist warned his hearers not to presume to say that they had Abraham as their father (Matthew 3:9). If a bloodline could not purchase standing for Israel, it purchases standing for no one.

Separation along racial lines is, to use a plain word, silly. It sorts people by a category Scripture does not use, one the bearer did not choose and cannot change. But it is not only silly, and Scripture does not treat it as merely foolish. When Peter withdrew from the table along ethnic lines, Paul opposed him publicly and said he was not in step with the truth of the gospel (Galatians 2:11–14). That was a gospel matter.

Culture is a different question, and a legitimate one. A nation is not merely a population inside a border; it is a people with a shared language, law, custom, memory, and worship. Those things are real and worth preserving, and a nation that loses them has stopped being that nation in all but name. Concern for cultural continuity is not a disguised racial concern.

The reason one question is legitimate and the other is not comes to this: a culture is something a person can be converted into, and an ancestry is not. A nation resting its identity on culture is open in principle to any newcomer willing to take it up; a nation resting its identity on blood is closed by definition. Grounding national identity in culture is not a gentler form of ethnic nationalism; it is what rules ethnic nationalism out.

Scripture shows the process. Ruth was a Moabitess, from a people under explicit covenantal exclusion (Deuteronomy 23:3), and she told Naomi, *“Your people shall be my people, and your God my God,”* culture and worship together. She ends in the line of David and in the genealogy of Christ. Rahab likewise. The sojourner laws assume the same movement. The stranger is treated as the native-born (Leviticus 19:34), and Exodus 12:48 is explicit: the sojourner who would keep the Passover must first be circumcised, and then he shall be as a native of the land. The door is open, one comes through it, and that costs something and takes time.

Two guards are needed. If culture is being used as a polite substitute for ancestry, it is the same error in better clothing, and I reject it in that form as well. And questions of how many a nation can absorb, and how quickly, belong to prudence and civic capacity rather than to human worth, which is what keeps them from collapsing back into what I have just rejected.

**No tribe outranks another before the throne of God, and no bloodline buys the mercy seat. Christ alone is King, and His kingdom gathers people from all nations.**
